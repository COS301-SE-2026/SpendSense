01

Foundation

Every design decision in this guide has been made according to 4 commitments agreed upon before development was started.

Purpose
Why we exist

To turn financial anxiety into financial confidence for students and young adults without the lecturing or shame of traditional financial apps.

Values
What we believe

Empowerment over judgement: every interaction respects the user. Learning over scolding: mistakes are growth, not failure. Fun over obligation if it isn't rewarding, we haven't designed it right.

Voice
How we sound, always

Your supportive friend who happens to be good with money. Encouraging, never preachy. We talk like a friend, not a banking email.

Tone
How we sound, situationally

Celebration messages are warm and encouraging. Reminders are gentle nudges. Errors are honest and helpful, never blaming.

02

Voice and tone in UI

Voice is the most important tool in the system. A well designed screen can still feel cold if the words are wrong. The examples below demonstrate how we leverage this tool to create a friendly, encouraging learning environment.

+Write it like this

Wins
Nice one! +25 XP for logging.
Specific reward, low-key celebration. The user feels seen, not patronised.
Reminders
Pssst, your phone bill is creeping up.
Playful and low stakes. It makes the user lean in rather than tense up.
Errors
Looks like this amount might be wrong. Try taking another look.
Names the issue, suggests the fix, never blames the user.
Empty states
Nothing here yet. Let's plant something.
Turns a blank screen into an invitation, not a void.

× Never like this

Lecturing
You should reduce your discretionary spending.
Authoritative, scolding, distant. Reads like a financial advisor.
Clinical
Transaction successfully recorded to your account.
Functional but soulless. We are not a bank, so we don't have to sound like one.
Blaming
Invalid input. Please correct and retry.
Cold, vague, and it feels like the user did something wrong.
Over-performing
Slayyy bestie, ur budget is budgeting
Trying too hard. Playful is not the same as performative.
03

Logo and Iconography

The SpendSense mark tells the whole brand story in one motion. A dot launching upwards pushing the two chevrons up to reach new heights, or in our case, new financial goals.

Anatomy of the mark

The chevrons
Two trajectories, one ascent

The upper chevron represents your goals, your future self. The lower chevron represents where you are and the dot is the energy pushing you upwards towards your goals.

The dot
A single deposit, a single decision

The dot represents one action launching you closer to your goals and resetting to do it all over again. Small actions, compounded, lift you up.

Sizing, clear space and forbidden treatments

RuleSpecification
Minimum size, full lockup120px wide on screen, 30mm in print. Below that, switch to the monogram.
Minimum size, monogram24px tall. The dot must remain visible as a distinct circle.
Clear spaceEqual to the height of the mark on all sides. Nothing enters that zone, including headlines and other logos.
ForbiddenNo rotation, drop shadows, glows, outlines, or gradients on the mark. No stretching the wordmark. No rearranging the chevrons horizontally. No placing the logo on a busy photo without a contrast plate. Never use the chevrons without the dot.

Icon Library

All product icons come from Lucide, drawn on a 24px grid with a 2px stroke, round caps and round joins. Icons render at three sizes: 24px standalone, 22px inside 48px category bubbles, and 18px inside 40px compact bubbles. Category bubbles are always circular and use the semantic colour of the category they represent.

Motion

SpendSense uses flowy, bouncy motions. The logo's animation sets the tone: a small action pushing the user up towards their goals. We've used spring physics on taps, count-up animations on number changes, and a slight squish on every press.

Accessibility

The conformance target is WCAG 2.2 AA, raised from 2.1 in Demo 1, with AAA achieved for all body text pairings as shown in the contrast table in section 04. The four principles below shaped all our decisions.

Perceivable
Can users sense the content?

Every text-on-background pairing is measured against the hex values in this guide, with the results published in section 04, including the pairings that fail.

Operable
Can users drive the interface?

All interactive elements are keyboard accessible in a logical tab order, including the floating action button and the bottom navigation. The focus indicator is a 2px navy outline with a 2px offset on every focusable component, visible against every surface in the palette. Touch targets are 44px minimum, and no interaction requires dragging, in line with the 2.2 dragging-movements criterion.

Understandable
Can users predict behaviour?

Navigation is consistent on every screen. Error messages are written in plain language that says what to do next, not just what went wrong. Form labels sit above their inputs, and placeholders are never the only label.

Robust
Will it work everywhere?

Semantic HTML throughout, with ARIA only where semantics fall short: labels on icon-only buttons, dialog roles on modals, live regions on toasts. The prefers-reduced-motion preference is respected across the app and on this page, with the logo animation and all in-app bounces fading or stopping for users who request it.
