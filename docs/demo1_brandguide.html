The brand foundation.

Every design decision starts here. Purpose, values, voice, and tone - the things you have to get right before you can pick a font.

PURPOSE
Why we exist

To turn financial anxiety into financial confidence for the generation that grew up with BNPL, subscriptions, and side hustles - without the lecturing, jargon, or shame of traditional finance apps.

VALUES
What we believe

Empowerment over judgment. Every interaction respects the user.
Learning over scolding. Mistakes are growth, not failure.
Joy over obligation. If it isn't rewarding, we haven't designed it right.

VOICE
How we sound, always

Your supportive friend who happens to be good with money. Warm, playful, encouraging, never preachy. We talk like a friend texting at 11pm, not a bank emailing at 9am.

TONE
How we sound, situationally

Celebration messages are warm and confetti-laden. Reminders are gentle nudges. Errors are honest and helpful, never blaming. Same friend, different moments.

// the human at the keyboard

How SpendSense talks.

Voice is the most leveraged tool in the system. A perfectly designed screen can still feel cold if the words are wrong. Here's how we get them right.

Do this

+ Wins
Nice one! +25 XP for logging.
Specific reward, low-key celebration. The user feels seen, not patronised.
+ Reminders
Pssst - your phone bill is creeping up.
Playful, low stakes, makes the user lean in rather than tense up.
+ Errors
Oops, that amount doesn't look right. Mind giving it another go?
Names the issue, suggests the fix, never blames the user.
+ Empty states
Nothing here yet - let's plant something.
Turns a blank screen into an invitation, not a void.

Not this

× Don't lecture
You should reduce your discretionary spending.
Authoritative, scolding, distant. Reads like a financial advisor billing by the hour.
× Don't be clinical
Transaction successfully recorded to your account.
Functional but soulless. We're not a bank - we don't have to sound like one.
× Don't blame
Invalid input. Please correct and retry.
Cold, vague, and feels like the user did something wrong. They didn't - the form did.
× Don't over-perform
Slayyy bestie, ur budget is budgeting
Trying too hard. Playful is not the same as performative.
// the mark

Logo & identity.

The SpendSense mark tells the whole brand story in one motion. A dot launches upward and bounces two chevrons higher than where they started. Spending becomes momentum. Setbacks become rebounds. The whole product, in a five-second loop.

Anatomy of the mark

// THE CHEVRONS

Two trajectories, one ascent

The upper chevron is the boldest stroke - your goals, your current self. The lower, lighter chevron is where you were before. Both move upward, but the dot's energy lifts the top one further. Progress is layered, not linear.

// THE DOT

A single deposit, a single decision

The dot represents one action - one expense logged, one bill paid on time, one quest completed. It launches, transfers its energy upward, and resets. The whole brand idea in five seconds: small actions, compounded, lift you.

// THE WORDMARK

Two voices, one identity

Spend is set in italic Georgia - soft, considered, almost confessional. Sense is set in bold sans-serif - confident, modern, decisive. The pairing says: we understand the weight of spending, and we'll bring sense to it.

// THE MOTION

Energy transfer in five seconds

The animation loop is intentional: dot rises and disappears, chevrons spring upward in sequence, then settle. It mirrors the in-app reward loop - log an expense, see the XP fly up, watch the score nudge higher. The logo previews the product.

Clear-space & usage rules

DO
Give it room to bounce

Maintain clear-space equal to the height of the mark on all sides. Use only the four approved colour pairings shown above. The wordmark always reads as one unit - italic Spend, bold Sense, never substituted. The chevrons always stack vertically with the dot below.

DON'T
Don't break the kinetics

Never rotate the mark, apply drop shadows or glows, stretch the wordmark, or rearrange the chevrons horizontally. Never place the logo on a busy photo without a contrast plate. Never use the chevrons without the dot - the dot is the kinetic anchor.

Typography.

Plus Jakarta Sans is the SpendSense typeface - geometric, friendly, with a chunky display weight that lets numbers dominate. Numbers are always the hero. Everything else supports them. The logo's "Spend" italic uses Georgia as a deliberate counterpoint - confined to the wordmark only.

The weight ladder

We use three weights and nothing else. 400 (regular) for body copy. 700 (bold) for headings and labels. 800 (extra bold) for display numbers - the hero moments. Numbers always dominate; copy supports.

Spacing & layout.

Base unit: 4px. Every margin, gap, and padding is a multiple of 4. The eye reads consistent spacing as designed; arbitrary spacing as rushed.

Corner radius scale

SpendSense uses mixed corner radii to create visual rhythm. Pills are fully rounded (999px). Cards are generous (20px). Buttons and inputs sit in between. The result: nothing in the UI feels sharp, but the silhouettes vary enough to stay interesting.

Motion.

SpendSense moves with bounce, never with stiffness. The logo's animation sets the tone: a small action, a launch, a settle. Every interaction in the product is a smaller version of that same loop. Spring physics on every tap, count-up animations on every number change, slight squish on every press.

Signature moments

A few specific animations carry the brand: the XP pill floating up on log, the score number counting upward after a payment, confetti bursts in palette colours on level-up, the flame growing when a streak day ticks over. These are the in-app cousins of the logo's bounce - small actions, visible momentum.

// the floor, not the ceiling

Accessibility.

SpendSense follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Accessibility isn't bolted on at the end - it's baked into every token, every component, every flow. The four pillars below guide every decision.

// PERCEIVABLE
Can users sense the content?

Every text-on-background combination tested to 4.5:1 minimum. Colour never carries meaning alone - paid status uses a check icon, bills due uses a label, not just a colour swatch.

// OPERABLE
Can users drive the interface?

All interactive elements keyboard-accessible. Visible focus rings on every focusable component (2px navy outline + 2px offset). The FAB and bottom nav are reachable by tab order, not just touch.

// UNDERSTANDABLE
Can users predict behaviour?

Consistent navigation across all screens. Plain-language error messages that tell the user what to do, not just what went wrong. Form labels above inputs - placeholders are never the only label.

// ROBUST
Will it work everywhere?

Semantic HTML always. ARIA labels on icon-only buttons. Reduced-motion preference respected - the logo animation and all in-app bounces fade or shorten for users who request it.
